These terms describe how Arclight sells and installs a curbside EV charging station, and what happens after we leave. The binding document is the signed installation agreement and service agreement, not this page. Where the two differ, the signed agreement wins.

## 1. What you are buying

A Level 2 EV charging station, installed at your property, plus the electrical work to make it run: the circuit, the trench and conduit, the disconnect, the permit, and the inspection.

**You own the station outright.** Title passes to you on final payment and completion of the install. It is not leased, financed, or licensed to you. You may sell it, move it at your own cost, resell the property with it, or stop using it. Arclight retains no interest in the hardware and no right to remove or disable it.

## 2. What Arclight provides after the install

We operate the payment layer: the card reader, the transaction, and the payout to you.

**Our fee is $1.00 per charging session plus 10% of the charge on that session.** Card processing is passed through at cost and is not marked up. There is no monthly fee, no fee on the hardware, and no minimum term. If your station sits idle for a month, you owe us nothing for that month.

## 3. What you are responsible for

- **The rate.** You set what drivers pay and you may change it at any time. We neither set nor approve your pricing.
- **The electricity.** The station runs on your service and your meter. That cost is yours.
- **Upkeep after the warranty period.** Cleaning, damage, and wear are yours.
- **Your tax position.** Charging revenue is income. We do not give tax advice; talk to your accountant before the first payout.
- **The site.** Keeping the space accessible and the station reachable for service.

## 6. Regulatory status of selling charging

<span class="not-prose inline-flex items-center rounded-md border border-amber-500/60 bg-amber-50 px-2 py-0.5 text-sm font-semibold uppercase tracking-wide text-amber-800">TBD: legal review</span>

Roughly forty states have passed a statutory exemption confirming that selling EV charging does not make you a regulated public utility. Illinois has not passed an equivalent provision that we are relying on; the Illinois Electric Vehicle Charging Act (765 ILCS 1085) addresses a tenant's and unit owner's right to install charging, not the regulatory status of reselling power.

We are not lawyers and this is not legal advice. Arclight makes no representation that operating a paid public charging station at your property is or is not a regulated activity in Illinois. Get your own advice before you switch it on.

## 7. Permits and the public way

Curbside work sits in the public right-of-way. That means a City of Chicago public-way permit in addition to the electrical permit, and it means **the parking space is not reserved for you or for charging** unless the city says otherwise. Anyone may park there.

If you are expecting a dedicated, reserved space, this product does not give you one. Say so before we quote.

## 8. Rebates

Rebate amounts, eligibility rules, and available funding are set by ComEd and by government programmes, not by us. They change without notice and can run out. Nothing on this site is a guarantee that you will receive any rebate, or that any figure quoted here will still be available when you apply.

## 9. Drivers

Drivers using your station are your customers, not ours. Disputes about your posted rate, availability, or the parking space are between you and them. We will supply session records to help you resolve them.
